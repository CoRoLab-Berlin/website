The [IFAF](https://www.ifaf-berlin.de/) funded project "[Exoskelette nach dem Prinzip elastischer Insektenlokomotion](https://www.ifaf-berlin.de/projekte/epi/)" (EPI) is a joint project of:

- [Berlin University of Applied Sciences](http://www.bht-berlin.de) working group softrobotics of Prof. Dr.-Ing. Ivo W. Boblan,
- [Berlin University of Applied Sciences](http://www.bht-berlin.de) working group medical physics of [Prof. Dr. Astrid Haibel](https://www.beuth-hochschule.de/people/detail/1313),
- [Hochschule für Technik und Wirtschaft Berlin](https://www.htw-berlin.de/) working group medical physics of [Prof. Dr.-Ing. Sebastian Götz](https://www.htw-berlin.de/hochschule/personen/person/?eid=10974),
- [Carl Stahl Hebetechnik GmbH](https://www.carlstahl-hebetechnik.de/),
- [Berliner Stadtreinigung](https://www.bsr.de/) Anstalt d.ö.R.,
- [Institut für Arbeitsschutz](https://www.dguv.de/ifa/index.jsp) bei der Deutschen gesetzlichen Unfallversicherung DGUV, and
- [Xploraytion GmbH](https://xploraytion.com/).

The project aims to use the bionics method to investigate the mechanism of the locust jump, extract the underlying operating principle, transfer it to technology, and adapt it to a support system (exoskeleton) for the human walking system.

The goal is to structurally and functionally decipher the catapult launch of the locust hind legs and to extend it over time in such a way that the energetic jumping mechanism can be translated into a lower-energy movement cycle for exoskeletons. This method of energy input must be human-like, ensuring the range of motion is unrestricted, the user experience is excellent, ergonomics are high, and safety is guaranteed for the wearer.

After decoding the locust jump, a mechanical replacement model is created, constructed in CAD, simulated under dynamic loads, and manufactured using 3D printing. Parallel to this, dynamic models of sensor-actuator coupling and suitable control concepts are developed, tested, and transferred to the control hardware of the support system. Field tests at the application partner and occupational safety analyses round off the project.

The lab is responsible for designing the construction, modeling, control, and supervision of the pneumatic drives for the exoskeleton.
