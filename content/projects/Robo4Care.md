The [IFAF funded project "Robotics for Care Enhancement" (Robo4Care)](https://www.ifaf-berlin.de/projekte/robo4care/) is a joint project of:

- Berlin University of Applied Sciences (BHT-B) working group Compliant Robotics Lab (CoRoLab) of Prof. Dr.-Ing. Ivo W. Boblan,
- Berlin University of Applied Sciences (BHT-H) working group Soft Interactive Robotics Laboratory (SIRo-Lab) of Prof. Dr.-Ing. Hannes Höppner,
- Alice Salomon University of Applied Sciences Berlin (ASH) working group Nursing Research of Prof. Dr. Johannes Gräske,
- HTW Berlin - University of Applied Sciences (HTW) working group Design and Drive Technology of Prof. Dr.-Ing. Matthias Führer,
- Fraunhofer Institute for Production Systems and Design Technology (IPK) working group Process Automation and Robotics of Dr.-Ing. Jan Kuschan and
- Fraunhofer Institute for Reliability and Microintegration (IZM) working group System on Flex of Malte von Krshiwoblozki
as well as the LOI partner
- Evangelisches Johannesstift Pflegen und Wohnen gGmbH (JSD),
- Institut für Arbeitsschutz der Deutschen Gesetzlichen Unfallversicherung e.V. (IFA),
- Deutsche Rentenversicherung Bund (DRB) Abteilung Prävention, Rehabilitation und Sozialmedizin and
- Berlin Partner für Wirtschaft und Technologie GmbH.

The overarching goal is to systematically reduce the workload of nursing staff in two key areas: (1) physically demanding lifting and holding tasks, and (2) time-consuming and technically challenging manipulation tasks, such as the daily preparation and dispensing of medications in nursing care.

In the two areas of focus, the following specific, measurable, and achievable sub-goals are defined:
- Development of a study design for the valid investigation of the acceptance, use, and benefits of body-worn exoskeletons in nursing (ASH)
- Development of a qualitative and quantitative metric for the comfort and UX (user experience) of exoskeletons in nursing to derive suggestions for improvement in the exoskeleton development process (BHT-B)
- Development of a measurement setup to measure relevant physical parameters of comfort when using exoskeletons, as well as recommendations for improving exoskeletons based on the developed metric (HTW)
- Development of a nursing-related scenario and study design to investigate the feasibility, safety, and acceptance of robotic assistance for handling tasks, e.g., medication administration (BHT-H, ASH)
- Development of a quantitative performance/quality metric for robotic handling tasks in nursing regarding success rate, error types, time required, manual intervention, and robustness. Tablet variants (BHT-H)
- Development and demonstration of an integrated robotics system consisting of an input station, a humanoid robot, and gripper hands for performing selected caregiving tasks, as well as experimental evaluation of the technical limits and operating conditions (BHT-H, HTW)
